Verified against sources available by November 8, 2026. This guide separates incidents and tells you which link supports which claim. Full claim-by-claim notes: [supply-chain incident research](/reports/cloudflare-npm/sources/npm-supply-chain-incidents/).

## Read these first

1. **[GitHub's official September 2025 response](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/our-plan-for-a-more-secure-npm-supply-chain/)**, September 22. Supports GitHub's September 14 Shai-Hulud notification, removal of 500+ compromised packages, malware upload blocking, and its announced authentication roadmap. The roadmap is a dated announcement, not proof all proposed controls were already deployed.
2. **[Qix's disclosure in the debug issue](https://github.com/debug-js/debug/issues/1005#issuecomment-3266868187)**, September 8, 2025. Primary maintainer disclosure linked by investigators. The fetched GitHub HTML did not expose its comments; use the [StepSecurity reproduction](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/20-popular-npm-packages-compromised-chalk-debug-strip-ansi-color-convert-wrap-ansi) for the verified phishing quotation, `support@npmjs.help`, wallet-interception explanation and eighteen original package/version pairs.
3. **[Wiz's September Shai-Hulud technical analysis](https://www.wiz.io/blog/shai-hulud-npm-supply-chain-attack)**, September 16, 2025. Supports post-install secret harvesting, GitHub-based exfiltration, malicious workflows and self-propagation through npm credentials. Its Nx/s1ngularity linkage is an assessment, not certainty about every original infection.
4. **[Datadog's Shai-Hulud 2.0 analysis](https://securitylabs.datadoghq.com/articles/shai-hulud-2.0-npm-worm/)**, November 25, updated December 4, 2025. Supports November 24 detection, pre-install Bun payloads, credential stealing, public-repository exfiltration, self-hosted runner persistence, destructive fallback, and its consolidated inventory of 796 package names and 1,092 versions. Those are November counts, not September counts.
5. **[PostHog's first-person November postmortem](https://posthog.com/blog/nov-24-shai-hulud-attack-post-mortem)**, November 26, 2025. Particularly strong: shows the external PR, mistaken `pull_request_target` trust model, stolen bot PAT, stolen npm token, and malicious publishing timeline. PostHog says it was directly seeded, rather than initially infected by the worm. Its recovery window applies to its own packages.
6. **[Microsoft's Mini Shai-Hulud May update](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/12/09/shai-hulud-2-0-guidance-for-detecting-investigating-and-defending-against-the-supply-chain-attack/)**. Originally December 9, 2025, explicitly updated May 13, 2026. Supports a distinct May resurgence, including a reported 170+ npm packages, two PyPI packages and 404 malicious versions. Do not date this update December 2025 just because of the URL.
7. **[Jared Wray's ChainDrop postmortem](https://jaredwray.com/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-chaindrop-worm)**, August 31, 2026. Best primary evidence for August 4: stale full-access GitHub PAT on an infected machine, poisoned Keyv/Cacheable/Ecto source, eleven original carrier versions, legitimate OIDC publication, valid provenance and remediation. He qualifies the PAT attribution and does not know with certainty how it was stolen. Quote: "Provenance proved where and how the package was built. It did not prove that the source was safe or that the change was authorized."
8. **[StepSecurity's ChainDrop analysis](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/chaindrop-npm-worm)**, August 4, updated 18:10 UTC. Supports the ChainDrop name, Bun preinstall loader, source/CI timeline, valid initial-wave OIDC provenance, Ethereum C2, developer-tool persistence and its snapshot of 444 package names / 2,212 versions. Includes lab telemetry and real Backstage CI execution evidence, expressly without confirmed credential loss.
9. **[Wiz's August analysis](https://www.wiz.io/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-npm-supply-chain-attack)**, August 4, 2026. Independent Mini-family analysis, over-400-package report, Ethereum contract-based C2-domain discovery, HTTPS/GitHub exfiltration, Claude/VS Code persistence, and selective revocation-watcher arming. Links an [exact-version CSV](https://github.com/wiz-sec-public/wiz-research-iocs/blob/main/reports/keyv-packages.csv).
10. **[Checkmarx's August technical analysis](https://checkmarx.com/zero-post/shai-hulud-npm-supply-chain-attack-keyv-cacheable/)**, August 5, 2026. Independent analysis with its own affected-version and IOC gists. Reports 443 names / 2,335 versions, not StepSecurity's 444 / 2,212. Preserve this disagreement; do not invent a combined total.

## What the evidence lets you say

- **Qix/chalk/debug and Shai-Hulud were different attacks.** September 8's code targeted browser cryptocurrency transactions. September's Shai-Hulud was an install-time credential-stealing worm. [Datadog comparison](https://securitylabs.datadoghq.com/articles/learnings-from-recent-npm-compromises/)
- **ChainDrop is not a fictional or inaccessible incident.** There is a full maintainer postmortem plus independent technical analyses. The initial compromised source was built and published by legitimate automation. [Wray](https://jaredwray.com/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-chaindrop-worm); [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/chaindrop-npm-worm)
- **OIDC/provenance were not shown to be cryptographically broken.** The compromised identity changed what the trusted workflow built. Correct provenance can faithfully attest a malicious commit. [Wray](https://jaredwray.com/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-chaindrop-worm)
- **Bun was an abused legitimate runtime.** The payload loader downloaded or used it to execute malware. That does not establish compromise of Bun's releases. [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/chaindrop-npm-worm)

## Numbers and wording to avoid

- Do not turn ordinary package weekly/monthly downloads into "people infected" or malicious-version downloads. Datadog separately reports more than 2.5 million malicious-version downloads for Qix's incident, but provides no reproducible counting dataset in that retrospective. Its reported $500 loss is also secondhand. [Datadog](https://securitylabs.datadoghq.com/articles/learnings-from-recent-npm-compromises/)
- Avoid a definitive eighteen-package final total: StepSecurity's eighteen-entry table coexists with its "20+" headline. Say "eighteen initially listed packages" or name examples. [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/20-popular-npm-packages-compromised-chalk-debug-strip-ansi-color-convert-wrap-ansi)
- Avoid an independently verified universal "two-hour" Qix window. Acknowledgement, registry removal, caches and shipped browser bundles have different lifetimes. The [Wiz report](https://www.wiz.io/blog/widespread-npm-supply-chain-attack-breaking-down-impact-scope-across-debug-chalk), found in search, itself describes acknowledgement followed by removal a few hours later as well as a two-hour timeframe.
- Avoid combining September's 500+ removal count, November's 796-name list, May's 404 versions, and August's 444-name / 2,212-version snapshot into one attack total. [GitHub](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/our-plan-for-a-more-secure-npm-supply-chain/); [Datadog](https://securitylabs.datadoghq.com/articles/shai-hulud-2.0-npm-worm/); [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2025/12/09/shai-hulud-2-0-guidance-for-detecting-investigating-and-defending-against-the-supply-chain-attack/); [StepSecurity](https://www.stepsecurity.io/blog/chaindrop-npm-worm)
- Avoid universal "opening the repository infects you" wording. The documented hooks are a persistence/execution path, but workspace trust, task approval and configuration matter. The evidence here does not establish every editor's trust controls were bypassed. [Wiz](https://www.wiz.io/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-npm-supply-chain-attack)

## Would another registry solve this?

**Potentially mitigable by different controls:** publication-token theft, phishing-sensitive account recovery, immediate adoption of fresh releases, malware distribution and insufficient artifact approval. Registry-side quarantine, strong authentication, scoped identities, cooldowns, explicit promotion and organizational proxies can reduce these risks. [GitHub](https://github.blog/security/supply-chain-security/our-plan-for-a-more-secure-npm-supply-chain/); [Checkmarx](https://checkmarx.com/zero-post/shai-hulud-npm-supply-chain-attack-keyv-cacheable/)

**Shared ecosystem risks:** compromised maintainer machines, writable source, overprivileged CI, legitimate identity building malicious commits, executable dependencies and transitive dependency trust. Mirroring the same tarballs elsewhere does not remove those properties. ChainDrop demonstrates why tokenless publishing and valid provenance remain useful but insufficient without independent source/artifact authorization and isolated execution. [Wray](https://jaredwray.com/blog/keyv-and-cacheable-chaindrop-worm); [PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/nov-24-shai-hulud-attack-post-mortem)
